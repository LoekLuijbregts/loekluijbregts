The Launch Is Too Late to Start Building Proof

Attention is most valuable when the story, evidence and next step are ready before the market looks. A launch date is the deadline for preparation, not the beginning of it.

By Loek Luijbregts · August 18, 2026 · 8 minute read

The press release is approved. The product page is nearly finished. Somebody creates a list of journalists. The launch is next Tuesday.

At that point, most of the outcome has already been determined.

A launch creates a short period in which customers, media, partners and investors may pay more attention than usual. If the value is still difficult to explain, the evidence is incomplete and nobody knows where interest should go next, the moment becomes expensive noise.

Strong launches build understanding and proof before they ask for attention.

News is not the same as a story

A company announcement answers: what happened?

A useful story also answers:

  • Why does this matter now?
  • Whose problem does it solve?
  • What makes the approach different?
  • What evidence reduces doubt?
  • Why should this audience care?

Journalists, creators and customers need enough substance to make their own decision. A louder claim cannot replace missing proof.

A six-week pre-launch proof system

Week 1: define the valuable change

Write one plain-language explanation of what becomes possible, easier or more valuable. Test it with people who are not inside the project.

Week 2: assemble evidence

Collect demonstrations, customer experiences, test results, credible users, commercial signals and the limitations you should state honestly.

Week 3: map trusted outsiders

Identify journalists, creators, experts, partners and customers whose attention would be relevant. A shorter list with a real reason to care is more useful than a large database.

Week 4: build the assets

Prepare photography, video, a concise fact sheet, founder answers, proof points and links. Make it easy for independent people to understand and verify the story.

Week 5: prepare commercial capture

Where will attention go? Build the subscriber page, product demonstration, investor information, lead form or retailer path before coverage arrives.

Week 6: brief, listen and adjust

Share the story under appropriate timing agreements, answer real questions and improve unclear parts. Early confusion is useful when there is still time to fix it.

The launch stack

Valuable change + credible evidence + relevant outsiders + useful assets + commercial capture + follow-up.

GRAVAA was ready when the result arrived

GRAVAA's adaptive tyre pressure technology became especially relevant in professional gravel and cobbled racing. The earned-media system was prepared before the biggest competitive moments.

That meant journalists already understood the technology and its relevance when major sporting proof arrived. The story did not have to be invented afterward.

The programme produced 30+ global media placements, tripled the social audience and added 9,000 subscribers.

Proof

GRAVAA: preparation turned high-profile product use into understandable international proof and a directly reachable audience.

Crowdfunding is also a proof launch

A public crowdfunding campaign does not only ask people for money. It asks them to understand the opportunity, trust the people behind it and believe others may see the same value.

For a current public crowdfunding launch, my role included positioning, the launch story and earned attention efforts. At the time of writing, the campaign had reached €350.000.

The figure matters, but so does the visible accumulation of belief. Each commitment becomes part of the evidence future participants evaluate.

This does not mean story alone caused the result. Product, team, terms, timing, network and campaign execution all matter. The strategic communication task is to make those real strengths understandable.

Prepare for success, not only launch day

Teams often plan how to generate attention and not what happens if they receive it.

  • Can the website handle and explain the interest?
  • Can sales respond quickly without changing the story?
  • Can journalists access accurate assets?
  • Can people subscribe if they are interested but not ready?
  • Can the company reuse the resulting proof in future conversations?

A launch should leave the business with more than a spike. It should create a stronger evidence base, a larger reachable audience and clearer knowledge about what the market values.

What to do if the launch is already close

Do not attempt to complete six weeks of work in two days. Prioritise three things:

  1. One clear statement of customer value.
  2. One credible proof point that can be independently understood.
  3. One useful destination for qualified attention.

Then treat the launch as the beginning of a longer proof programme rather than the only day that matters.

The real launch question

Do not ask only: “How do we make people look?”

Ask: “When the right people look, what will help them understand, believe and act?”

The answer needs to exist before launch day.

Preparing a launch people need to understand?

Bring the proposition, proof, outside voices and commercial path together before the attention window opens.
