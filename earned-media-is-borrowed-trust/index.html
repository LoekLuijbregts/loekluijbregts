Earned Media Is Not Free Reach. It Is Borrowed Trust

The most valuable press, creator and partnership exposure does more than put a logo in front of people. It lets a credible outsider make your value easier to believe.

By Loek Luijbregts · Published 18 August 2026 · 8 minute read

Earned media is often introduced as the free alternative to advertising.

That definition makes it sound cheap, tactical and mainly useful for generating impressions. It also hides the reason earned attention can be unusually valuable for a business that is difficult to understand.

An advertisement says the company believes its own promise. Earned media shows that a journalist, creator, expert, partner, customer or respected participant found the story worth carrying.

The value is not simply reach. It is borrowed trust.

Trust only transfers when the connection makes sense

A famous name beside a product does not automatically create credibility. Trust transfers when four conditions are present.

  1. Relevant authority: the person or publication is respected by the audience that matters.
  2. Genuine connection: their involvement is logical, not a decorative endorsement.
  3. Visible proof: the audience can see what they tested, judged, selected or experienced.
  4. Commercial path: interested people can take a useful next step.

Remove relevance and the attention is broad but weak. Remove proof and it feels promotional. Remove the commercial path and the moment disappears without creating an asset.

Chris Burkard and the value of the right participant

For the 10th edition of the Mark Gunter Photo Awards, I supported the positioning and earned press exposure efforts and helped attract legendary photographer Chris Burkard to the jury.

His value was not simply that many people know his name. He represented exactly the international outdoor photography credibility the awards wanted to reinforce.

The right person can communicate seriousness faster than another paragraph of self-description. Their participation becomes proof that respected people inside the field consider the initiative worth their time.

GRAVAA needed belief, not more product claims

GRAVAA produces a technically complex adaptive tyre pressure system. The engineering is sophisticated, but technical sophistication does not automatically create market understanding.

Professional race use and international cycling media gave the product a context people trusted. The technology was not merely described by the manufacturer. It was being used in situations where tyre pressure could materially influence performance.

The resulting work produced:

  • 30+ global media placements
  • a social audience that grew by a factor of three
  • 9,000 new subscribers

The placements created visibility. The professional context created credibility. The subscriber growth turned part of that attention into a direct relationship.

The Borrowed Trust model

Four connected decisions

Authority → relevance → proof → capture. Who already has the audience's trust? Why should they care? What will the audience be able to see? Where does qualified attention go next?

Start with the customer, not the media list. Which outside voices already help them decide what is credible?

Then identify the proof those voices need. A journalist cannot create a strong story from a vague ambition. A creator cannot demonstrate a product that is not ready. An expert should not be asked to endorse something they have not meaningfully experienced.

Earned media works best when the business already contains something worth discovering: a real innovation, surprising outcome, useful customer insight, strong human story or credible event.

Why earned does not mean effortless

No media invoice does not mean no investment.

Strong earned programmes require preparation, access, relationships, evidence, assets, timing and follow-up. They also require the courage to let an independent person judge whether the story is worthwhile.

This is precisely why earned attention can create more trust than controlled promotion. The company does not own the decision.

Do not leave the value inside the publication

Coverage becomes more valuable when the business reuses it responsibly.

  • Add relevant placements to product and investor pages.
  • Give sales teams a concise proof collection.
  • Build follow-up content that answers questions the coverage created.
  • Invite interested people to subscribe, register, enquire or see a demonstration.
  • Track the quality of resulting conversations, not only the audience estimate.

The publication owns its audience. You can still build a durable proof asset from the moment and create an honest route into your own customer relationship.

The better earned-media question

Do not begin with “How can we get more press?”

Ask: “Whose independent attention would make our most valuable truth easier for the right customer to believe?”

That question produces a different story, a different partner list and a much more useful definition of success.
