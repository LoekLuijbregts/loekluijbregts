Generosity Is a Growth System When It Creates Better Proof

Generosity creates commercial value when it helps good people do better work, makes trust visible and leaves behind evidence that can compound.

By Loek Luijbregts · Published 12 June 2026 · Updated 18 August 2026 · 9 minute read

Some of the most valuable work I have done began before anyone discussed a scope.

I shared an observation, connected two people, helped a creator see their commercial value or brought a credible person into an idea that deserved more attention.

That does not mean giving away strategy forever. It means recognising that useful generosity can improve the opportunity itself.

Three forms of valuable generosity

1. Give recognition

Tell someone specifically what is valuable in their work. Many talented founders and creators are surrounded by broad praise but lack precise evidence about why their best customers care.

Recognition can become strategic input when it names the strength, the customer and the consequence.

2. Give useful access

A thoughtful introduction can save months of cold outreach. The introduction only creates value when the fit is real, both sides understand why they should meet and the receiver protects the trust being transferred.

3. Give a first piece of judgment

Share the one observation that helps someone frame the problem more accurately. Do not perform the entire project. Help them see what deserves attention.

The generous growth test

Does this action help a good person create better work, make a better decision or reach a more relevant audience? Can I offer it without neglecting an existing promise?

Generosity changed the quality of the proof

For the tenth edition of the Mark Gunter Photo Awards, I supported positioning and earned-press efforts and helped attract legendary adventure photographer Chris Burkard to the jury.

The value was not the famous name by itself. His participation strengthened the meaning of the award, increased confidence around the initiative and gave media and participants a more compelling reason to pay attention.

A relevant contribution from a respected person can become proof that other people use to evaluate the whole project.

The network is not a contact list

A valuable network is a history of well-kept promises. People remember whether your introduction had context, whether you claimed credit and whether you continued to care after the exciting moment.

This is especially important in the Creator Economy. Creators, brands, destinations and media partners operate with different incentives. The person who can see mutual value and protect all sides becomes more useful than the person with the largest database.

Make the contribution visible without making it transactional

Generosity compounds when the resulting proof can be understood. Document the collaboration with permission. Explain what each person contributed. Share the customer result. Credit people precisely.

This is not a demand for public repayment. It is good knowledge management. Future customers and collaborators can only learn from value that is made visible.

Protect the boundary

Unbounded generosity becomes hidden labour. It can also train people to request your judgment without valuing the time required to produce it.

Use three boundaries:

  1. Time: offer a clear amount of help.
  2. Depth: distinguish a useful observation from a full diagnosis.
  3. Responsibility: do not promise an outcome you are not engaged to deliver.

A generous “here is the most important thing I notice” can sit beside an honest “solving it properly would require a project.”

A monthly generosity practice

  • Give one person precise recognition for valuable work.
  • Make one well-framed introduction with permission from both sides.
  • Share one resource or observation that helps a customer decide.
  • Credit one collaborator publicly and specifically.
  • Document one outcome so the proof can keep working.

This is not a funnel. It is a habit that makes you more useful and your network more capable.

The return is larger than leads

Generosity can create referrals, partnerships and revenue. It can also create access, learning, reputation and the freedom to work with people you respect.

The strongest return may arrive indirectly. Someone you helped becomes the person who sees a future opportunity and knows exactly why you should be part of it.

Give where you have genuine leverage. Protect the trust. Make the resulting value visible. That is how generosity becomes a growth system without becoming a performance.
