The Founder’s Lean Social Media Growth Stack

Most founders do not need more social media software. They need a small system that helps them capture useful ideas, publish clearly, start conversations and keep the relationship.

By Loek Luijbregts · January 15, 2026 · 8 minute read

The easiest way to waste money on social media is to buy tools before deciding what the system needs to do.

A scheduling tool cannot rescue a weak idea. An automation cannot make an irrelevant offer valuable. A video platform cannot replace a useful point of view. Software can remove friction, but only after you understand the movement you want to create.

For most founder-led businesses, the job is smaller than it looks. You need to move from lived experience to a useful story, from that story to a conversation, and from that conversation to a relationship you can continue outside the feed.

The four jobs

Capture the raw material. Create the clearest version. Convert interest into a conversation. Keep permission to continue the relationship. If a tool does not improve one of those jobs, it probably does not belong in the stack.

1. Capture: use the tools already in your pocket

Your best raw material is rarely a blank content calendar. It is the customer question you answered this morning, the mistake you noticed in a proposal, the sentence that changed a meeting or the photo that proves the work happened.

Start with voice notes, camera roll favourites and one running note called “Things worth explaining.” Give each observation a rough label: question, proof, mistake, belief or story. The capture system should be so simple that it still works on a busy day.

2. Create: reduce the distance between idea and publication

Some people think better on paper. Others become clear when speaking. If video helps you explain, a lightweight recording workflow is more useful than another complicated production setup.

You still need a useful idea, but the path from notes to a clear explanation becomes shorter.

Do not turn every thought into video. Choose the format that preserves the idea with the least distortion. A strong paragraph may be better than a talking head. A customer screenshot may be stronger than either.

3. Convert: give attention somewhere useful to go

A post can create a lot of visible engagement and very little business movement. The missing step is often a relevant next action.

ManyChat can automate parts of the conversation that follow a comment or direct message. That is useful when the promised response is specific: a checklist, route, example, booking link or short diagnostic. It is not permission to spam people who showed a moment of interest.

The automation should feel like good service at scale. Deliver what you promised immediately. Ask one useful question. Make it easy to stop. Move people to email or a real conversation only when that next step is relevant.

A commercial distinction

Engagement is not permission. A like is a platform signal. A requested resource, email opt-in, qualified reply or booked conversation is a customer action. Measure them differently.

4. Keep: build an audience you can reach again

Social platforms are excellent discovery engines, but the relationship remains partly rented. Algorithms change. Accounts are restricted. Formats lose reach. A useful stack therefore ends with an owned record of genuine permission: an email subscriber, CRM contact, customer community or direct commercial conversation.

This is not an argument to drag everyone into a funnel. It is a reminder that attention becomes more valuable when the customer chooses to continue.

The smallest useful weekly system

  1. Save three real questions or observations during the week.
  2. Turn the strongest one into one clear post or short video.
  3. Offer one relevant next step, not five generic calls to action.
  4. Record replies, opt-ins and qualified conversations.
  5. Use those responses to choose next week’s subject.

That loop is more valuable than publishing every day without learning. The tools should make the loop easier to run, not make the operation look more sophisticated.

Buy the bottleneck, not the promise

If ideas disappear before you capture them, fix capture. If knowledge becomes stiff on camera, fix creation. If posts generate interest but no action, fix conversion. If every conversation ends inside the platform, fix retention.

A lean growth stack does not make the founder less human. It protects the human parts by automating only the friction around them.
