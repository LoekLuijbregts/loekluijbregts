Teach Before You Pitch, Without Giving Away the Work

Useful teaching helps customers recognise the problem and judge the options. It does not require you to perform their entire diagnosis and implementation for free.

By Loek Luijbregts · Published 6 May 2026 · Updated 18 August 2026 · 8 minute read

Many experts sit between two unhelpful fears.

If they share too little, their marketing sounds vague. Customers cannot see the quality of their thinking, so the expert asks for trust before offering evidence.

If they share too much, they fear customers will copy the method and never buy.

The useful middle is to teach the customer how to see and decide, while charging for depth, adaptation, execution and responsibility.

Information is not the whole service

A recipe is not a restaurant. A training plan is not a coach. A positioning framework is not the judgment required to choose which customer evidence matters and what the business should stop doing.

Expert services create value through several layers:

  • recognising the real problem;
  • choosing among competing truths;
  • adapting the work to context;
  • creating alignment and momentum;
  • executing to a professional standard;
  • and carrying responsibility for the result.

You can teach a useful principle without providing all six layers for free.

The boundary

Teach recognition and decision quality. Sell tailored diagnosis, implementation, access and responsibility.

What to teach publicly

Teach the expensive misunderstanding

Explain the assumption that causes customers to waste money or choose the wrong solution. A clear distinction demonstrates judgment.

Teach the questions

Give customers a way to inspect their situation. For example: What valuable result do customers mention that the proposition does not? Which proof resolves the biggest risk? What only becomes clear after talking to the founder?

Teach the standard

Show what good work contains and why each part matters. This helps customers compare offers on value instead of price alone.

Teach from proof

Use a real case to explain what changed, what the team did and what the result does or does not prove. Specificity builds trust more effectively than universal claims.

What remains paid

Context. The same framework produces different choices for a founder, a family business and a tourism board.

Prioritisation. Customers rarely lack possible actions. They need help choosing the one that creates the most valuable movement now.

Craft. Knowing what a strong story contains does not automatically create the interview, copy, film, media pitch or customer journey.

Accountability. Valuable work is completed, tested and improved under real constraints.

Network and access. Trusted introductions and the judgment to use them well cannot be downloaded from a post.

Why teaching attracts better clients

The wrong-fit prospect may take the checklist and leave. The right-fit customer uses it to understand why the problem matters, why shortcuts fail and where experienced help creates leverage.

Build a diagnostic asset

Create one short article, scorecard, quiz or workshop around a decision your ideal customer regularly gets wrong. A useful diagnostic should:

  1. name the costly pattern;
  2. help the customer recognise it;
  3. show the consequences of leaving it unresolved;
  4. offer a valuable first action;
  5. and explain when outside help becomes useful.

This is not a disguised sales pitch. If the customer never hires you, the diagnostic should still improve their decision.

Do not turn generosity into an endless audit

There is a difference between answering a useful question and performing several hours of bespoke analysis without agreement. State the boundary kindly.

You might say: “I can explain the principle and show you what I would inspect. Applying it to all five markets requires a proper diagnostic.”

A clear boundary protects both sides. The customer knows what they are buying. The expert can remain genuinely generous without building resentment.

The pitch becomes smaller when the teaching is better

Useful content allows the customer to arrive with shared language, realistic expectations and evidence of how you think. The sales conversation can focus on their context rather than proving that you know the subject.

Teach enough to make the problem visible. Show enough proof to make your judgment credible. Charge for helping the customer turn that understanding into a result.
